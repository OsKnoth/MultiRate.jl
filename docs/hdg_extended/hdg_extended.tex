\begin{abstract}
LMARS is written with the intermediate (interface) momentum: on each side of a face the interface pressure is given by
the pressure inside the element and the interface momentum, and LMARS is the condition that the left and the right
interface pressure are equal. Taking the interface momentum as a new unknown on the faces, as in hybridized DG, the
DGSEM becomes a set of element equations, each depending only on the unknowns of its element and on the interface
momentum, together with this algebraic constraint. The constraint is relaxed: the time derivative of the interface
momentum equals a relaxation parameter times the constraint. In the fast system the tendencies of the thermodynamic
variables then depend only on the momentum, inside the element and on the faces.
\end{abstract}

\section{Fast system}
Variables $(\rho,\ m=\rho\mathbf u,\ \Theta=\rho\theta)$, coefficients frozen at a state (\code{docs/frozen\_dg}): $\theta$, $A=P'(\Theta)=\gamma p/\Theta$,
$c^2=A\theta$, pressure $p=A\Theta$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_t+\nabla\cdot m=0,\qquad \Theta_t+\nabla\cdot(\theta\,m)=0,\qquad m_t+\nabla p+g\rho\,\mathbf e_z=0 .\label{eq:fast}
\end{equation}
Thermodynamic variables $a=(\rho,\Theta)$, momentum $b=m$. With the pressure as additional variable (\code{DGPressureModel}) the
$p$ row is $A$ times the $\Theta$ row; it is not written separately.

\section{LMARS with the intermediate momentum}
At a face with unit normal $n$ from the left state $L$ to the right state $R$, the normal acoustics of \eqref{eq:fast} with frozen
coefficients is
\[
p_t+c^2\,\partial_n m_n=0,\qquad (m_n)_t+\partial_n p=0
\]
($p_t=A\Theta_t=-A\theta\,\partial_nm_n$, $c$ the frozen face sound speed), with the characteristic speeds $\pm c$ and the Riemann invariants
$p\pm c\,m_n$. In the Riemann problem the invariant $p+c\,m_n$ is carried from the left, $p-c\,m_n$ from the right into the
intermediate state $(m^*,p^*)$:
\[
p^*+c\,m^*=p_L+c\,m_{n,L},\qquad p^*-c\,m^*=p_R-c\,m_{n,R}.
\]
For a given intermediate momentum $m^*$ each relation defines an interface pressure of its side, from the pressure inside
the element and the intermediate momentum:
\begin{equation}
P_L^*=p_L-c\,(m^*-m_{n,L}),\qquad P_R^*=p_R+c\,(m^*-m_{n,R}).\label{eq:sides}
\end{equation}
LMARS is the condition that the two interface pressures agree,
\begin{equation}
P_L^*=P_R^*,\label{eq:equal}
\end{equation}
which gives, with $\jump x=x_R-x_L$, $\avg x=\tfrac12(x_L+x_R)$,
\begin{equation}
m^*=\avg{m_n}-\frac{\jump p}{2c},\qquad p^*=P_L^*=P_R^*=\avg p-\frac c2\jump{m_n}.\label{eq:lmars}
\end{equation}
With the outward normal $\nu$ of an element $K$ ($\nu=n$ for $L$, $\nu=-n$ for $R$), $m_\nu=\nu\cdot m$ and $m^*_\nu$ the intermediate momentum
in the direction $\nu$ ($m^*_\nu=m^*$ for $L$, $-m^*$ for $R$), both relations \eqref{eq:sides} are one formula,
\begin{equation}
P_K^*=p_K-c\,(m^*_\nu-m_{\nu,K}).\label{eq:side}
\end{equation}
At a wall $m^*=0$, so $P_K^*=p_K+c\,m_{\nu,K}$ (the LMARS wall pressure; no condition).

\section{DGSEM with LMARS}
Tensor LGL nodes, collocated quadrature, element-local derivatives $\nabla_h$ (SBP), mass $J\,w$ per node. At a face node of
element $K$ with outward contravariant normal $\tilde n$, $s=|\tilde n|$, $\nu=\tilde n/s$ and
\[
\sigma=\frac{s}{J\,w}\qquad(w\text{ the quadrature weight of the face node in the normal direction}),
\]
the strong form with the LMARS values reads
\begin{align}
\rho_t&=-\nabla_h\cdot m-\sigma\,(m^*_\nu-m_\nu),\nonumber\\
\Theta_t&=-\nabla_h\cdot(\theta m)-\sigma\,(\theta_{\rm up}\,m^*_\nu-\theta\,m_\nu),\label{eq:dg}\\
m_t&=-\nabla_hp-g\rho\,\mathbf e_z-\sigma\,\nu\,(P_K^*-p),\nonumber
\end{align}
$\theta_{\rm up}$ the frozen upwind value; at interior nodes only the volume terms act. By \eqref{eq:side} the face term of the momentum is
\[
-\sigma\,\nu\,(P_K^*-p_K)=\sigma\,c\,\nu\,(m^*_\nu-m_{\nu,K}):
\]
it contains no pressure, only the intermediate momentum and the momentum of the element.

\section{The interface momentum as additional variable}
On every face node introduce one unknown $\hat M$, the interface momentum (single-valued, in the direction of the face normal
$n$; $\hat M_\nu=\pm\hat M$ seen from $K$), and use it in place of $m^*$. With $\hat P_K=p_K-c\,(\hat M_\nu-m_{\nu,K})$ the system becomes, as in
hybridized DG, element equations and a constraint on the faces:
\begin{align}
\rho_t&=-\nabla_h\cdot m-\sigma\,(\hat M_\nu-m_\nu),\nonumber\\
\Theta_t&=-\nabla_h\cdot(\theta m)-\sigma\,(\theta_{\rm up}\,\hat M_\nu-\theta\,m_\nu),\label{eq:hdg}\\
m_t&=-\nabla_hp-g\rho\,\mathbf e_z+\sigma\,c\,\nu\,(\hat M_\nu-m_\nu),\nonumber\\
0&=\hat P_L-\hat P_R=p_L-p_R+c\,(m_{n,L}+m_{n,R})-2c\,\hat M\quad(\text{interior faces}),\qquad \hat M=0\quad(\text{walls}).\label{eq:constraint}
\end{align}
Each element equation depends only on the unknowns of its element and on the interface momentum of its faces; the
elements are coupled only through $\hat M$. The constraint \eqref{eq:constraint} is the equality \eqref{eq:equal} of the left and right interface
pressures, local to the face node and linear in $\hat M$; solved for $\hat M$ it gives $\hat M=m^*$, and \eqref{eq:dg} is recovered. Conservation of
mass and $\Theta$ holds for every value of $\hat M$, since $\hat M$ is single-valued.

\section{Relaxation of the constraint}
The algebraic constraint is replaced by a relaxation equation: the time derivative of the interface momentum equals a
relaxation parameter $\kappa>0$ times the constraint,
\begin{equation}
\hat M_t=\kappa\,(\hat P_L-\hat P_R)=\kappa\,\big(p_L-p_R+c\,(m_{n,L}+m_{n,R})-2c\,\hat M\big).\label{eq:relax}
\end{equation}
Since $\hat P_L-\hat P_R=2c\,(m^*-\hat M)$, the interface momentum relaxes towards the LMARS value with the time $\varepsilon=1/(2c\kappa)$. The
system \eqref{eq:hdg}, \eqref{eq:relax} is an ODE in the element unknowns and the interface momentum; for $\kappa\to\infty$ it tends to the DAE
\eqref{eq:hdg}--\eqref{eq:constraint}, i.e.\ to DGSEM with LMARS.

\section{Structure}
With the element unknowns $a$, $b$, the face unknowns $\hat M$ and the forcing $f$ of the slow part, the fast system reads
\begin{align*}
a_t&=K\,b+R\,\hat M+f_a,\\
b_t&=L\,a+D\,b+Q\,\hat M+f_b,\\
\hat M_t&=\kappa\,(H_a\,a+H_b\,b-2c\,\hat M),
\end{align*}
with
\begin{itemize}
\item $K$: the volume divergence of $m$ (and $\theta m$) and the face terms $+\sigma m_\nu$, $+\sigma\theta m_\nu$ of the element's own momentum;
  $R$: the face terms $-\sigma\hat M_\nu$, $-\sigma\theta_{\rm up}\hat M_\nu$;
\item $L$: pressure gradient and gravity, volume terms only; $D$: the face term $-\sigma c\,\nu\,m_\nu$ of the node's own momentum; $Q$: the face
  term $+\sigma c\,\nu\,\hat M_\nu$;
\item $H_a$: $p_L-p_R$, $H_b$: $c\,(m_{n,L}+m_{n,R})$, both local to the two nodes of the face node pair.
\end{itemize}
$K$, $L$ and $D$ act within an element ($D$ within a face node), $R$ and $Q$ connect a face node with the two element nodes
at it, and only the constraint connects the two sides of a face. The thermodynamic tendencies depend only on the
element momentum $b$ and the interface momentum $\hat M$, i.e.\ in the fast system only on momentum; they contain no
thermodynamic variable. The pressure jump, which in DGSEM-LMARS couples $a$ with $a$ across the face, enters only the
equation of the interface momentum. Ordered as $(a,\ b,\ \hat M)$:
\[
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\\\hat M\end{pmatrix}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&K&R\\L&D&Q\\\kappa H_a&\kappa H_b&-2c\kappa I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\\\hat M\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}f_a\\f_b\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
On the gravity-wave grids the faces split into horizontal ($\xi$) and vertical ($\eta$, walls) ones; $\hat M$ on $\xi$-faces belongs to
the horizontal part, on $\eta$-faces and walls to the vertical part of a node column.

\section{Fast step: Störmer--Verlet in the groups $a$ and $(b,\hat M)$}
\paragraph{Groups.} Collect the momentum and the interface momentum in $Y=(b,\hat M)$. The fast system is
\begin{equation}
a_t=K_Y\,Y+f_a,\qquad Y_t=G\,a+D_Y\,Y+f_Y,\label{eq:groups}
\end{equation}
\[
K_Y=\begin{pmatrix}K&R\end{pmatrix},\qquad G=\begin{pmatrix}L\\\kappa H_a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_Y=\begin{pmatrix}D&Q\\\kappa H_b&-2c\kappa I\end{pmatrix},\qquad f_Y=\begin{pmatrix}f_b\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is the form of Verwer's semi-discrete Maxwell equations \cite{verwer}: the first block row has no self-coupling, the
second has the self-coupling $D_Y$ (his conductivity). $D_Y$ is local: it couples the momenta of the two nodes of a face node pair
with the interface momentum of that pair ($D$ acts on the node's own momentum, $Q$ and $H_b$ connect node and face); in 2D the
corner nodes of an element link the pairs of a $\xi$- and an $\eta$-face.

\paragraph{Step.} Störmer--Verlet in the two groups (Xu, van der Vegt, Bokhove \cite{xvb}, eq.~(4.6), without the Coriolis term),
with the self-coupling of the second group treated by the trapezoidal rule as in Verwer's component splitting:
\begin{align}
a^{1/2}&=a^n+\tfrac h2\big(K_YY^n+f_a\big),\nonumber\\
\big(I-\tfrac h2D_Y\big)Y^{n+1}&=\big(I+\tfrac h2D_Y\big)Y^n+h\big(G\,a^{1/2}+f_Y\big),\label{eq:sv}\\
a^{n+1}&=a^{1/2}+\tfrac h2\big(K_YY^{n+1}+f_a\big).\nonumber
\end{align}
The system for $Y^{n+1}$ is local (blocks of a face node pair) and needs no global solve. With $D_Y$ explicit instead,
$Y^{n+1}=Y^n+h(G\,a^{1/2}+D_YY^n+f_Y)$, the step is the plain Störmer--Verlet step. Properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item For the trapezoidal rule the step is symmetric: second order. The half steps of consecutive small steps can be combined.
\item Steady states are kept exactly: a state with $K_YY+f_a=0$ and $Ga+D_YY+f_Y=0$ is a fixed point of all three lines. The forcing
  $Ga+f_Y$ and the damping $D_Y$ are in the same line.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{1D results} (periodic acoustics, DGSEM $P=4$, 16 elements, $c=1$, $\Delta x_e=1$, $\varepsilon=1/(2c\kappa)$ in units of $\Delta x_e/c$;
\code{docs/hdg\_extended/fast\_groups\_1d.jl}). The extended system \eqref{eq:hdg}, \eqref{eq:relax} has no growing modes ($\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\le10^{-15}$ for
$\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$, $0.1$). Stability limit $c\,h/\Delta x_e$ of the step and model error of the extended system against DGSEM-LMARS
(smooth wave of wavelength 4 elements, relative error of $p$ after two periods):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}\toprule
$\varepsilon$ & $10^{-4}$ & $10^{-3}$ & $10^{-2}$ & 0.05 & 0.1 & 0.2\\\midrule
\eqref{eq:sv}, $D_Y$ trapezoidal & 0.004 & 0.014 & 0.043 & 0.085 & 0.106 & 0.123\\
plain Verlet, $D_Y$ explicit & 0.000 & 0.002 & 0.015 & 0.040 & 0.052 & 0.061\\
model error & -- & $6.4\cdot10^{-3}$ & $6.4\cdot10^{-2}$ & 0.32 & 0.62 & 1.2\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For comparison, explicit Störmer--Verlet for DGSEM-LMARS (the limit $\varepsilon\to0$, $\hat M$ eliminated) is stable up to 0.075. The
symplectic Euler form ($Y$ step, then a full step in $a$) has the same limits as the Verlet form. The trapezoidal step is of second
order (error ratios 4.0/4.0 for $\varepsilon=0.05$ against the exact flow of the extended system). Both forms are limited by the
relaxation $-2c\kappa=-1/\varepsilon$ in $D_Y$: explicitly it needs $h\lesssim\varepsilon$; with the trapezoidal rule the amplification
$(1-\tfrac h2\lambda)^{-1}(1+\tfrac h2\lambda)$ of the stiff relaxation $\lambda\approx-1/\varepsilon$ tends to $-1$, and the step is stable only for $h$ of the
order of a few $\varepsilon$ up to the limit of the coupled system. A step larger than the explicit LMARS step needs $\varepsilon\gtrsim0.05\,\Delta x_e/c$,
and then the extended system differs from DGSEM-LMARS in proportion to $\varepsilon$: the interface momentum relaxes towards
$\avg{m_n}-\jump p/(2c)$ and so lags behind the central mass flux $\avg{m_n}$ as well (a phase error of the waves).
